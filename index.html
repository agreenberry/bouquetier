The method

  • Order of placement. Greenery sets the outline, line flowers set height and width, the focal bloom goes in at the visual centre, secondary blooms ring it, filler softens the gaps, then small sprigs are tucked into any space still showing, accents go in last.
  • Odd numbers. Every variety comes in ones, threes, fives or sevens, except the focal group, which is built for depth instead.
  • The focal group. Light: the main bloom and one small one. Full: the main bloom, a partner about three-quarters its size below it on one side, and one small one lower on the other side, drawn in close to the group but never directly underneath. The three make a loose triangle: no corner sharper than 30° or wider than 115°. Heavy: the main bloom, two full-size partners (one set well apart), two small ones placed diagonally (one high, and one in a consistent pocket just out from the main bloom and a little below it, above the far partner so it stays in view), and a third smaller one just under the main bloom to fill the gap between the lower partners. Small blooms are tucked behind the main one, a shade deeper, peeking out.
  • Round means round. The round posy fills a dome that sits on its stems, rather than fanning out from the ribbon. Greenery tips may poke just past the dome's edge.
  • Balance. The focal group is centred on the main bloom's line. Each secondary bloom, accent and filler then goes wherever it best evens out the weight left and right, so neither side is left bare.
  • Size steps down outward. The largest bloom sits lowest and most central; line flowers and accents work the edges.
  • 60 · 30 · 10. The key colour leads, a supporting colour follows, an accent stays near a tenth. White and green are neutrals.
  • Harmony on the wheel. Hues are read from the palette's hex values: analogous within 60–95°, split-complement 105–145°, complement 145° and beyond, triadic near 120°.
  • Banned pairs. Poppy with Cosmos, Poppy with Crimson bud, Marigold with Crimson bud.
  • Parts coloured separately. Petals vary in tint inside a bloom, centres contrast, lines take the deeper shade of the part they border.
  • Flowers point to the sky. Heads tilt with their stems; no leaf points more than 95° from upright.
